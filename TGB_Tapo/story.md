# TGB Tapo

Not everything in the garage needs a story about a mountain pass. Some vehicles earn their place by removing friction from ordinary days, and the Tapo was one of those.

A scooter is the most honest form of transport there is. No gears to think about, no ceremony, no warm up. You turn a key, you twist a grip, you arrive. In city traffic that simplicity turns into a genuine advantage: gaps that a car spends five minutes queuing for take about four seconds.

The Tapo asked for almost nothing in return. It started. It ran. It sat outside in weather that would have made something more precious sulk, and then started again. Over time that reliability changes how you plan a day, because you stop building contingencies around whether the transport will cooperate.

There is a version of this write up that apologises for the Tapo not being exciting. I would rather not. Most of the tools I rely on daily are unremarkable, and that is precisely why I rely on them.

It was never going to be the bike anyone asked about. It was the one that was always ready to go.
